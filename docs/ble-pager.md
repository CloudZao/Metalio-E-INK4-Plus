# BLE page turners

[中文](ble-pager_zn.md) | **English**

## Connection and device management

Page turner settings beside Bluetooth group the auto-connect switch and its description under Page turner settings: nearby page turners are found automatically, and auto-connect turns off if none is found. The current connection row shows its label on the left and the page turner name or Not connected on the right. Devices opens a dedicated full-screen manager using the Wi-Fi configuration page’s grouped, outlined and selected styles. Saved devices show Connected, Nearby or Not nearby; a separate group lists nearby unsaved devices and availability without signal values. Saved rows open a centered dialog. Disconnected devices offer Connect and Forget; connected devices offer Disconnect and Forget, plus Customize buttons when button learning is supported. Tapping an unsaved device connects directly, switching away from an existing connection when needed. Connecting a saved device marked Not nearby only shows a top notification; no connection attempt is made. Failed manual connections keep the list and failure status visible without starting a scan; the user can retry or reopen Devices to scan again. Connection or nearby status on the right is vertically centered against the two-line device name and address group on the left. Opening Devices starts a five-second scan with progress centered in the content area without a border. After the first round, device groups stay visible. Each completed round is followed by a three-second pause before the next five-second scan. Background discoveries update their rows immediately; results not seen during a round are removed when it finishes. Signal changes alone do not rebuild the list. Opening a device dialog, connecting, disconnecting, forgetting a device, entering standby or leaving the page stops scanning and further rounds. Reopening Devices starts discovery again. Connection attempts keep the device list visible and show Connecting in the target device row. Fixed areas do not scroll; each device group scrolls only when its contents overflow. Downward swipes open the global menu in fixed areas and empty or short lists; lists with a vertical scroll range retain scrolling gestures. A HOME click goes back one level; holding HOME returns to the home screen. Tapping outside closes the dialog. Returning to settings preserves the Page turner tab, and leaving the page preserves the connection.

## Automatic connection and persistence

The auto-connect switch is persistent. When enabled, startup and each standby exit begin a 30-second search window. Nearby saved devices take priority over recognizable unsaved devices; stronger signals take priority within each group, and failed candidates yield to others. Manual connection enables the switch and attempts only the selected device without starting an automatic search window. The switch remains on after success. If no usable connection is established within the search window, the switch turns off and saves that state. Turning it off cancels scanning and connection attempts and disconnects the active device. Explicit disconnect and forgetting the active device also turn auto-connect off to prevent an immediate reconnection. Standby pauses activity while preserving the switch; waking resumes the search when enabled. Manual scans do not change the switch. Saved devices have no fixed count limit and use storage sized to the actual list. Existing devices and button mappings migrate automatically, and failed saves preserve the previous configuration. When the stack’s pairing-key cache fills, its native replacement policy applies; saved devices and mappings remain available, and devices can pair again on reconnection. Scans deduplicate by address and show up to six results.

## Key learning

Connected Free 2, Free3-R, Smart 1-E, HBTR002-X and HBTR003-X devices support button mapping for Previous page and Next page. Defaults are A for previous and B for next; learning is optional. Button mapping and learning use the same centered dialog, sized to fit its content and identifying the device and action being configured. Tap the target button, then save, learn again or clear the mapping. Learning times out after 20 seconds. The BLE task saves changes and returns to the mapping page on completion; failure preserves the old mapping and prompts a retry. Learning isolates business input. The two actions cannot share an input; replacement is shown before saving. Each device keeps separate settings. Free 2 dispatches clicks only, without long-press recognition, page repeat, recording or BOOT mappings.

## Protocols and resource lifecycle

The S31 configuration in `sdkconfig.defaults.esp32s31` places SDK-supported wireless stack BSS and NimBLE host allocations in PSRAM. Wi-Fi IRAM, RX IRAM and extra IRAM acceleration are disabled to leave internal RAM for both radio stacks and DMA; Wi-Fi throughput may be lower.

The page turner uses the on-chip BLE Central independently of the UART Bluetooth audio module. For Free 2, it discovers service `0x1812`, checks up to eight notification-capable `0x2A4D` characteristics and reads their `0x2908` Report Reference descriptors. All Input reports (type `1`) are subscribed by writing `01 00` to their `0x2902` descriptors, and raw notifications from every report are logged. Only validated Report IDs `1`, `3` and `4` are decoded as Free 2 buttons; other reports support protocol identification without triggering page turns or button learning. No-input/no-output pairing is attempted when encryption is required. Reports are identified by descriptors rather than fixed handles, allowing different page turners to use different report IDs. Each connection selects the first report carrying a valid nonzero button as its input channel, so zero values from other reports do not interfere with key deduplication.

Free 2 Report ID `1` uses single-byte direction commands: `01` defaults to previous page and `02` to next page. Each notification triggers one click without waiting for a release report, including consecutive identical commands. These inputs also support the existing previous/next button learning. Report IDs `3` and `4` use three-byte notifications: `01 00 00` is button A, `02 00 00` is button B, and `00 00 00` clears the protocol key state. Repeated nonzero state within one press dispatches only once; a new nonzero state after a zero report is a new click. Other lengths, combined button bits and nonzero reserved bytes are ignored without changing key state.

A separate bounded queue delivers input to the UI. `pager_key_handler` calls the existing bezel click handler, `VkKey_Dispatch`. Every page retains its existing previous/next click behavior, including back navigation and page turns; pages without a matching action retain their existing behavior. BLE does not call individual application pages. Page changes invalidate queued input. Learning, disconnect and standby invalidate pending input; queue overflow clears pending events. BLE stops and releases its stack before manual light sleep, so connections do not survive light sleep. The page turner cannot wake the reader; automatic reconnection after standby follows the switch.

Before initializing BLE, the cleanup worker is started and retained with a PSRAM stack, so releasing the stack does not require another internal RAM task allocation. It returns to normal idle shutdown after BLE is released. The switch preference is saved by the internal-RAM storage worker; NVS writes do not run on the PSRAM cleanup stack. Turning the switch off or reaching the automatic connection timeout fully deinitializes BLE in the background. With the switch off, leaving manual discovery also releases the stack. Enabling or scanning again reinitializes BLE; saved devices and button bindings are retained.

Free3-R subscribes to HID Input reports and retains raw logs, decoding only Report ID `3`. Three-byte notifications default to previous page for `02 00 00`, next page for `01 00 00`, and standby for `08 00 00`. An all-zero report clears the button state; repeated reports within one press do not retrigger actions. `00 02 00` and other unrecognized reports are logged without dispatching actions or changing deduplication state. The standby key is excluded from page button learning. Standby uses the existing power-key click handler, retaining OTA and USB ownership checks and transfer cleanup. Page turner input is ignored while already in standby.

Smart 1-E is identified by its advertised name and supports discovery, connection, saved device management and automatic reconnection. Report ID `1` uses single-byte commands: `01` defaults to previous page, `02` to next page, and `06` enters standby. Each notification triggers one click without waiting for a release report; other data is logged without dispatch. Page buttons support learning; the standby key is fixed. Raw notifications from all HID Input reports remain logged.

HBTR002-X and HBTR003-X share the gesture protocol. Report ID `2` carries four-byte gesture reports: the first byte `07` indicates contact, and `02` or `00` ends contact; the remaining bytes pack 12-bit horizontal and vertical coordinates. Increasing horizontal coordinates default to previous page and decreasing coordinates to next page. One completed horizontal gesture produces one click; small movements, vertical gestures and incomplete data do not turn pages. Report ID `1` uses `00 10` for standby and `00 00` for release. Page buttons support learning; the standby key is fixed. Disconnect, device switching and input cancellation clear incomplete gestures.

BLE DEBUG logs retain every received raw notification, including connection handle, characteristic handle, Report ID, length and all hexadecimal bytes. Longer reports are logged in chunks without a report-count limit. Scan rounds and raw notifications use DEBUG. Normal logs retain device discovery, connection, report IDs and types, notification subscription handles, disconnect reason, dispatched actions and mapping saves to correlate raw reports with dispatched actions.
